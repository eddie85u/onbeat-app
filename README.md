# Onbeat

A drum practice app for a practice pad, the computer keyboard or an electronic drum kit. A staff with a moving line shows what to play; with a kit or the keyboard, every hit is graded for timing, the right pad, and loud or soft. Lessons go from your first quarter notes to double kick and odd time, and you can play along to songs, whole or one part at a time.

## Kit, keyboard or practice pad
- **Electronic kit:** plug the drum module into the computer with a USB cable and choose "electronic kit" when you first open Onbeat (or in Settings). Onbeat finds it on its own and grades every hit. In Chrome, allow Onbeat to use MIDI devices when it asks.
- **No kit yet:** the computer keyboard works as drums (the keys are shown on the kit picture).
- **Practice pad or acoustic drums:** choose "practice pad or acoustic drums" when you first open Onbeat (or in Settings). Press **Listen** to have the exercise play over and over with the drums while you play along, or **Start** for a single run and say whether it was clean. Works on a phone, tablet or TV browser too.

## Installing the Mac app
1. Download `Onbeat-mac.zip` from the link above and double-click it. Drag **Onbeat** into your **Applications** folder.
2. Open it. The first time, macOS says it cannot check the app, because Onbeat is not from the App Store. Click **Done**.
3. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down to the message about Onbeat and click **Open Anyway**, then confirm.
4. From then on it opens normally. Keep it in the Dock by dragging it there.

New versions install over the old one; your progress stays.

## Installing the Windows app
1. Download `Onbeat-windows.zip` from the link above. Right-click it, choose **Extract All**, and put the folder where you like (Documents, say).
2. Open **Onbeat.exe** in that folder. The first time, Windows says **Windows protected your PC**, because Onbeat is not from the Microsoft Store: click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. It asks only once.
3. To keep it handy, right-click Onbeat.exe and choose **Pin to Start** or **Pin to taskbar**.

For a new version, extract the new zip over the old folder; your progress stays (it is kept in `%APPDATA%\onbeat`, not in the folder).

## Songs
Songs are in the desktop app (Mac or Windows); the web version is for practice. Onbeat comes with no songs. A song is a folder with a drum chart (`notes.mid` or `notes.chart`) and its `song.ini`, plus the song's audio files if you have them; drag the folder onto the **Songs** screen. Charts made by fans for Clone Hero and YARG work.

## Your profile on several devices
Onbeat is free, and your progress stays on each device. Donate US$10 (40,000 pesos) or more and you get a code that unlocks one cloud profile: saved online and kept in step on up to 3 devices (TV, phone, computer): see **[Donate to Onbeat](https://eddie85u.github.io/onbeat-app/support.html)**. Type the code in **Settings → Sync**, then link the other devices there with a QR code or a short link code.

## Your data
Your progress stays on your device: no accounts, no ads, no tracking, and nothing about your playing is sent anywhere, unless you turn on sync for a profile; then that profile's progress (no names or emails) is kept on Onbeat's sync service for your other devices. Onbeat loads everything it needs from this site; the only other service it contacts is YouTube, and only when you pick a video for a song in the desktop app. The web version keeps your progress in Chrome on that computer; **Settings → Backup** saves it to a file you can move to another computer or to the desktop app.
